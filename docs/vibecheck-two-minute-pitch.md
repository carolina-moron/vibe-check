# VibeCheck — Two-Minute Pitch

## Something feels off? Check the vibe.

Every day, someone receives a message that looks like an opportunity: a job abroad, a work-from-home offer, a student-visa arrangement, a relationship, or an investment. It may begin with easy money and friendly conversation. Then come the fees, the urgency, the request for a passport, the secret location, or the pressure to stop asking questions.

By the time people recognise the danger, they may have lost money, documents, freedom, or contact with the people who could help them.

**VibeCheck is a free, open-source second opinion before that happens.** Anyone can check a message, screenshot, profile, job offer, housing offer, travel invitation, or suspicious organisation. VibeCheck looks for the warning signs that appear across real scam and trafficking cases: upfront fees, unusually high pay, secrecy, isolation, document control, pressure, tied housing, and travel arranged by strangers.

It does not pretend to know everything. It does not check private individuals or call anyone a trafficker. It shows an explainable score, the evidence behind every warning sign, what could and could not be verified, and where to get confidential help. No result says “clear,” because the absence of a public record is not proof of safety.

The site connects three things that are usually separated:

1. **Prevention:** plain-language guides, digital-safety education, cybersecurity resources, and practical “know your rights” information.
2. **Understanding:** sourced case journeys, news patterns, survivor-informed stories, and country context from the Forced Labor Structural Risk Index.
3. **Action:** verified hotlines and organisations, anonymous reporting, and a check people can run before they pay, send documents, travel, or reply.

VibeCheck is also designed for the people helping others: nonprofit organisers, families, career offices, educators, and platforms. Its agent can check public job postings, queue concerning results for human review, and draft reports. A person approves every report; the system does not make accusations automatically.

We are building this with frontline and community partners, including Apne Aap Women Worldwide, Ethical Tech CoLab, and Women in Cloud. The next step is to connect more safety and public-record APIs, expand education and multilingual resources, publish more documented cases, and build a privacy-preserving evidence base that helps researchers and practitioners see patterns without exposing victims or making unverified claims.

**VibeCheck moves the intervention earlier — from “what happened to me?” to “does this feel safe before I take the next step?”**

Visit **https://carolina-moron.github.io/vibe-check/** and check the vibe.
