# Six days a week in the fields

“In Mexico the contractor told me about legal farm work in the United States on an H-2A visa. The pay was good, the hours were fair and my expenses would be paid back. To get the job I had to pay recruitment fees, and they were big.”

“In Florida none of it was true. We worked long days, six or seven days a week, for far less than we were owed. They kept our passports. They said we owed them money, and the debt kept growing. We slept in crowded, dirty housing and were moved from farm to farm: Kentucky, Indiana, Georgia, North Carolina. When anyone complained, they threatened us with arrest, with deportation, and with harm to our families back home.”

**How it came out.** Federal prosecutors charged the company's leaders under the racketeering law, treating the contractor as a criminal enterprise. The company's owner, Bladimir Moreno, was sentenced to 118 months in prison in 2022, and other defendants received 41 months, 37 months and 8 months of home detention. Alexander Villatoro Moreno pleaded guilty to RICO conspiracy, and in June 2026 a court in the Middle District of Florida sentenced him to 70 months.

## Warning signs
- Large recruitment fees for a visa job. On H-2A the employer, not the worker, is meant to pay these.
- Someone else keeps your passport.
- A debt to your boss that you can never pay off.
- Threats against your family or of deportation.
