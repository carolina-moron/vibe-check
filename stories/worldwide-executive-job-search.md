# The executive recruiter on LinkedIn

*Dramatised retelling of the FTC case against Worldwide Executive Job Search Solutions, 2019. The narrator is a composite, not a real person.*

I'd been job hunting for months when the LinkedIn message came. A recruiter said my background qualified me for executive roles at private equity and venture capital firms. The jobs weren't advertised anywhere, they said, but they had relationships with hundreds of firms.

To get an interview I had to pay a fee first: somewhere between $1,200 and $2,500. They also offered to fix my résumé, for another fee. It felt like the price of getting in the door at that level, so I paid.

The interviews never became jobs. The jobs, it turned out, weren't real.

**How it came out.** The Federal Trade Commission sued. In February 2019 a federal court in Texas froze the company's assets. In October 2019 the owner, Craig Chrest, and his companies settled. They were banned for life from selling employment services, with a $1,744,422 judgment, all but $18,000 of it suspended.

## Warning signs
- A recruiter contacts you, not the other way round, about jobs nobody else can see.
- You pay before you get an interview. Real recruiters are paid by the employer.
- Chat-only contact with no way to verify the firms they name.
