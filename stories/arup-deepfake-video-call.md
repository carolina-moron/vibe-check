# The CFO on the video call

*Dramatised retelling of the Arup deepfake case, Hong Kong, 2024. The narrator is a composite, not the real employee.*

I work in finance in our Hong Kong office. One day a message came, apparently from our chief financial officer in the UK. He needed help with a confidential transaction.

I had doubts. A secret deal over email felt wrong. So when they set up a video conference, I was relieved. There was the CFO, and other colleagues I recognised. Faces I knew, voices I knew. They walked me through it, and I made the transfers they asked for: several payments to five Hong Kong bank accounts, about US$25 million in total.

Not one person on that call was real. The CFO and the others were generated by AI.

**How it came out.** Afterwards the fraud was discovered. Arup reported it to Hong Kong police in January 2024 and later confirmed to the Financial Times that false voices and images had been used. As far as public reporting shows, no one has been charged.

## Warning signs
- A "confidential" or secret payment request from someone senior.
- A video call is used to calm your doubts. Seeing a face is no longer proof.
- Pressure to act during the call instead of checking afterwards.

## What would have helped
- Hang up and call the person back on a number you already have, not one from the message.
- Ask something only the real person would know, or use an agreed code word.
- Follow a rule that large transfers always need a second person to approve through a separate channel.
