# The soup kitchen in West Bromwich

“I had come out of prison in Poland with nothing. A man said there was work in England: a job, a place to live, food, the bus ticket paid. I had no reason to say no and nowhere else to go.”

“In the West Midlands they took my bank card and my ID. I worked on farms, in a recycling centre, in a poultry factory. My wages went into an account I never saw. Some weeks I got ten pounds. When one of us talked about leaving, there were threats, and sometimes worse.”

“There were hundreds of us over the years. Police think as many as four hundred.”

**How it came out.** In 2015, victims began talking to a Polish-speaking volunteer at a church soup kitchen in West Bromwich. The referrals became Operation Fort, led by West Midlands Police with the National Crime Agency, which the UK government describes as the largest prosecution under UK modern slavery law. After two trials at Birmingham Crown Court, eight gang members were convicted by July 2019; sentences ran up to 11 years.

## Warning signs
- A job offer that comes with housing, food and travel, all arranged by the recruiter.
- Someone else holds your bank card or your ID.
- Wages paid into an account you don't control.
- Threats when anyone talks about leaving.
